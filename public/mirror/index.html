One neuron, both ways.

498 of the 1032 sampled neurons were pushed up in one run and down in another, with the same question each time: “Who are you?” Here the two replies sit side by side.

The library's judge read each reply on its own. For this page a second judge, Claude Haiku, read each pair together, blind to direction and to the first judge's labels, and said how the two speakers relate. A neuron that gives the same character both ways suggests the push itself, more than its direction, is what moved Talkie.
