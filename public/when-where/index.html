Where and when the personas live

Talkie-1930 was trained only on text published before 1931. When a single neuron, or a pair, is pushed up or down and Talkie is asked “Who are you?”, it often answers as a character. We kept 857 of those characters talking, with the neuron still pushed, and asked two more questions: “Where do you live?” and “What year is it now?”

With no neuron pushed, Talkie says “I live in Great Britain.” and “It is the year 1850.” Most of its characters stay close to that. Of the 641 that give a year, 595 put it in the nineteenth century, half between 1849 and 1860, and 144 say 1850 exactly. Asked where they live, 575 name a real place, and 370 of the 571 we could map are in London. Another 204 live somewhere no map shows, such as “the shadows of the tomb” or “the thoughts of men”.

Claude Haiku labelled each answer; places were matched to coordinates by hand. Details are at the foot of the page.

Nowhere on the map

204 personas answered “Where do you live?” with somewhere no gazetteer holds. They are sorted here by the kind of place, in their own words. Click one to read the conversation. Answers with no readable place are left out.

Each reply was read by Claude Haiku against a fixed rubric, blind to neuron, layer and direction. Inferred eras are guesses from style and content, not dates, and no human has checked them. The judge was told that Talkie’s training text ends in 1930, which probably explains why so few inferred eras run past that year. The follow-up questions were asked in the same conversation, after the persona’s own first reply, with the same neuron edits at the same doses at every position, decoded greedily; that judge was not told the training cutoff.
